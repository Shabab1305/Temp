01 The Museum

A country remembers itself in the objects it refuses to let go of — a rifle, a wireless set, a folded flag, the hull of a tank that still carries the marks of the rounds that hit it.

The museum was established in 1987 at Mirpur Cantonment and moved in 1992 to Bijoy Sarani, onto the ground beside the planetarium. In January 2022 it reopened there as a purpose-built ten-acre complex, and after August 2024 it returned to its original name: the Bangladesh Military Museum.

The building is organised as three wings — Army, Navy and Air Force — around a central plaza. Galleries run from the military history of this delta, through the Liberation War of 1971, to the modern services and Bangladesh's long record in United Nations peacekeeping. Augmented and virtual reality displays sit alongside the objects themselves. Outside, armour, artillery and aircraft stand in the open air around a landscaped waterbody.

Designed by Ali Imam and Bayejid Mahbub Khondker with Nakshabid Architects, Design Work Group, Mukta Dinwiddie MacLaren Architects and Foster Lomas Architects; exhibition design by Real Studios.

02 Nine Months

1971

7 March

The Racecourse Speech

At the Ramna Racecourse, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman addresses a crowd of over a million. "The struggle this time is the struggle for our freedom." The words function as a declaration in everything but name.

25 March

Operation Searchlight

A military crackdown begins in Dhaka overnight. Universities, police lines and residential neighbourhoods are attacked. The war of independence begins in the dark, and the exodus toward the borders starts within days.

17 April

The Mujibnagar Government

In a mango grove at Baidyanathtala, the provisional government of Bangladesh is sworn in. The war acquires a state: a cabinet, a command structure, and eleven numbered sectors of resistance.

April — November

Sectors and the Mukti Bahini

Eleven sectors, regular brigades and thousands of guerrilla fighters. Bridges, rail lines and river craft become the battleground; the Naval commandos of Operation Jackpot strike shipping in Chittagong and Chalna.

3 December

The Final Offensive

Allied forces of Bangladesh and India advance on Dhaka from every direction. Airfields are neutralised in the first days; armoured columns bypass strongpoints and drive for the capital.

16 December

Victory Day

At the Ramna Racecourse — the same ground as March — the instrument of surrender is signed. Bangladesh is independent. The date gives its name to Bijoy Sarani, the avenue this museum stands on.

03 Inside

The Galleries

Arranged over a basement, ground, mezzanine and two upper floors, with the central plaza and its escalators running through the middle of the building.

01

Bangladesh History Gallery

Ground floor

Military tradition on the Bengal delta — riverine warfare, the colonial regiments, and the men from this land who fought in wars that were not their own.

02

Army Gallery · 1971

First floor

The largest of the service galleries, and the one that carries the Liberation War: weapons carried by the Mukti Bahini, sector maps, the wireless equipment of Swadhin Bangla Betar Kendra, and the personal effects of those who did not come home.

03

Navy Gallery

Basement

From the commando raids of Operation Jackpot to the modern fleet. The basement levels take the Navy's larger artefacts, below the plaza.

04

Air Force Gallery

Second floor

The Kilo Flight of 1971 — a Dakota, an Otter and an Alouette turned into a wartime air arm — and the aircraft, radars and equipment that followed it.

05

UN Peacekeeping & the Hill Tracts

Mezzanine

Bangladesh has been among the largest contributors of personnel to United Nations peacekeeping for decades. The mezzanine follows those deployments, alongside the armed forces' work in the Chittagong Hill Tracts.

04 Open Air

The Outdoor Exhibits

Armour

Tanks and Armour

Among them a captured T-54, its armour still carrying every bullet strike and shrapnel gash it took in combat. Walk the length of a hull, look up at a turret ring, and an armoured advance stops being an abstraction on a map.

Air

Combat Aircraft

Fighters and trainers preserved on their stands, wings held at the angle of a turn they will never finish. The delta wing overhead is the clearest signature in the museum's skyline.

Artillery

Guns and Launchers

Field pieces, anti-aircraft mounts and their carriages, set out around the waterbody so the mechanisms of elevation, traverse and recoil stay visible rather than hidden behind glass.

Water

Naval Craft

A vessel out of its element, hull dry and open to the weather — the closest most visitors will come to standing on the deck of a warship built for these rivers.

05 Within the Complex

Toshakhana & the Victory Arena

Both open off the ground floor, on either side of the central plaza. The Toshakhana is a museum in its own right — five storeys, built by the Bangladesh Army — holding the gifts and honours presented to the state and its senior officers.
